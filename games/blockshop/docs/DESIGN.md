# Blockshop visual direction

Blockshop should look like a small, obsessively organized neighborhood
workshop—not a generic neon brick-breaker screen.

## Materials

- Warm cream pegboard and pale drafting paper.
- Painted blocks in tomato, mustard, cobalt, mint, lilac, and orange.
- Visible plywood grain on hardwood blocks and the catch tray.
- Cool steel bearings, brackets, screws, and unbreakable blocks.
- Masking-tape labels and condensed black workshop lettering.

## Readability rules

1. The material communicates hits: flat paint is one, wood is two, steel is
   permanent.
2. A first hit cracks hardwood without changing its silhouette.
3. Every power uses one shape, color, abbreviation, and label everywhere.
4. The ball remains the brightest moving object.
5. Decorative tools stay outside the collision field or behind a low-contrast
   pegboard layer.

## Motion

Blocks kick backward and shed a few square paint chips when broken. The catch
tray compresses on impact. A collected power tag stamps its label beside the
HUD, then its timer drains like a strip of masking tape. Multiball must remain
readable: each bearing gets a short, fading graphite trail.

Reduced-motion mode removes camera kick, block rotation, and decorative motion
while preserving ball travel and all rule-bearing state.

## Sound

Painted blocks make a dry wood knock, hardwood a lower clack, and steel a short
bell. The bearing ticks against walls, the tray produces a firm table-tennis
return, and power tags use brief mechanical sounds rather than musical fanfare.
